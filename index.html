The playbook, decoded

Real estate in Melbourne, commission-only. Every deal is really two sales.

First, win the listing: find the vendor, earn the trust, stay in touch — sometimes for years. "Hey Christine, remember me?" Pitch. List. Hold the line on fee.

Then the sale itself starts: opens, calls, finding the right buyer, negotiating hard enough that both sides still shake hands after. "Congrats, Emma." Champagne.

Then it starts again: referrals from past clients, conveyancers, brokers, other agents. Coffee on Tuesday with Jake. Thirty to thirty-five of these a year, on repeat, for five years.

None of that is luck. It's the same skill on loop: build trust, keep it, and be the person someone calls back to when they're finally ready to act.

Why ActivePipe, why now

Maintaining contact with an ever-growing database is hard — especially staying relevant and top of mind once it's a few hundred names deep. I've used ActivePipe myself as an agent: the insight it gives into how a campaign is actually performing, and the ability to tailor communication instead of sending the same email to everyone, is what most agents are missing without it.

Nothing hurts more than seeing a listing go up on a street where you'd already had contact with that owner — and then watching a competitor's board go up in front of their house because you weren't the one who stayed in touch. That's not a hypothetical, it's happened to me and to every agent I know. That's the exact gap ActivePipe closes, which is why I'd be selling something I already believe in, not learning to believe in it on the job.

I am French. I am used to making the first move.
